This appendix documents the E5 runs behind \cref{sec:robust}: what was run, further results of
the grid and of the alternative split, the simulated power of the critic under different
optimiser settings (\cref{tab:e5-simpower}) and the architecture ablation
(\cref{tab:e5-arch,tab:e5-arch-real}).

\subsection{What was run}

The settings below are those of the full configuration recorded with the results; where the
docstring of \texttt{experiments/e5\_robustness.py} gives other replication counts, it describes
the plan before the compute cuts. The \emph{grid} covers the default and 13 one-at-a-time changes,
five generators and the five synthetic seeds of E3, with the absolute \WC{} test and the anchored
relative test under the dependent protocol (time-block split, Driscoll--Kraay p-values). The
\emph{split} part refits the five generators on the alternative \TRAIN{} period and runs the full
E3 battery: the \WC{}, \LRC{} and C2ST tests with Driscoll--Kraay p-values, MMD and \SigMMD{} with
the wild bootstrap, KS (invalid under dependence), and the anchored and two-critic relative tests,
with five seeds. The power and the boundary size of the rule at this split come from the
replication functions of E3 for D1 and D2, with 25 replications of five seeds each (the docstring
says 50). The \emph{simpower} part runs 50 replications per cell (the docstring says 100).

Three parts were not run. \emph{gridsize} would have estimated the size of the absolute test in
each configuration from 200 placebo replications in each of two simulated designs, with an exact
comparison against the default on the paired rejections. \emph{gridpower} and \emph{gridrealvol}
would have estimated the power and boundary size of the rule in each configuration under D1 and
D2, with 50 replications per design. The calibration table planned for this appendix therefore
does not exist, and the column ``Rule power'' of \cref{tab:e5-sensitivity} is empty. Above all,
a change of decision in the grid cannot be attributed to a change of size or to a change of
power. Only the default pipeline has a measured size: in the factor-panel placebo of E1 with
common volatility regimes, the absolute \WC{} test rejects at 0.085 [0.054, 0.132] and the
anchored test at 0.080 [0.050, 0.126] at nominal 5\% when the synthetic panel is a joint draw,
and at 0.270 [0.213, 0.335] and 0.155 [0.111, 0.212] when the synthetic tickers are drawn
independently with the same margins, the situation of the univariate generators of E3
(\cref{tab:e1-size-sim}).

The architecture ablation (\texttt{experiments/e5\_architecture.py}, results in
\texttt{results/e5arch}) has two parts. On simulated data it reruns the \WC{} test with the
pooled critic in ten cells of E2 ($n=500$; 200 replications in each of the two null cells, which
were first run with 50 replications and then extended, and 50 in each of the eight alternative
cells) and two alternative cells of E2-dep ($n=2000$; 50
replications each). On real data it reruns the E3
protocol with E3's five seeds, and designs D1 and D2 with 20 replications of five seeds each,
paired with the first 20 replications of E3. No part of E1 was rerun with the pooled critic.

\subsection{Grid: sample sizes, lags and seeds}

In the default configuration each sample has 3000 \TEST{} windows, of which the absolute test
evaluates 1500; the anchored test evaluates 1500 synthetic windows and, depending on which time
blocks a seed assigns to the evaluation half, 3000 to 3200 of the 6200 \TRAIN{} windows. With
$L=5$, $10$ and $60$ a sample has 12300, 6100 and 1000 \TEST{} windows, of which the absolute test
evaluates 6000 to 6300, 3000 to 3100 and 500. With $L=60$ each ticker has 10 \TEST{} windows, so
with $K=10$ every time block holds a single window date and each half five dates. Training
fractions 0.3 and 0.7 leave 2100 and 900 evaluation windows per sample in the absolute test,
against 1500 by default. The Andrews plug-in, applied to each of the two evaluation samples
separately, selects 0 to 4 lags (in window dates) in the absolute test and 0 to 7 in the
anchored test of the default configuration; the Newey--West rule selects 2 and 2 or 3, and
changes no decision.

The seed combination is not a formality. In the default configuration the five single-seed
anchored p-values of GBM are 0.403, 0.177, $2.4\times10^{-4}$, $2.4\times10^{-10}$ and
$2.2\times10^{-6}$; three of the five seeds reject on their own for GBM, Merton and Heston, and one
for \GARCHt. With five seeds, $\pmed\le0.05$ requires the third smallest $p_s$ to be at most
$0.025$ (\cref{rem:combine-seeds}). An analysis with a single seed would have reached opposite
conclusions for GBM depending on the seed.

\subsection{Alternative split}

The alternative split has 43 \TEST{} windows and 50 \TRAIN{} windows per ticker (4300 and 5000 per
sample). The fit diagnostics resemble those of E3 except for Heston. The Merton intensity
$\lambda_J$ is at its bound for 3 tickers at both splits, and the \GARCHt{} persistence reaches its
cap for no ticker, against one in E3. The Heston simulated-moments fit has at least one parameter
at a bound for 52 tickers ($c$ for 27, $\kappa_v$ for 4, $\rho_v$ for 32), against 33 in E3 (11, 5
and 20).

Among the absolute tests at the alternative split (\cref{tab:e5-split}), the \WC{}, \LRC{} and
C2ST tests and MMD reject only GBM. \SigMMD{} rejects GBM, Merton and Heston ($\pmed$ 0.004,
0.004 and 0.012), as it does at the main split (\cref{tab:e3-pooled-tests}). KS rejects every
generator but is invalid. No valid test rejects \GARCHt{} or the stationary bootstrap. The
secondary two-critic test rejects only GBM ($\pmed=0.012$).

For the rule, the single-seed rejection rates are 1.00 under the D1 alternative and 0.12 [0.03,
0.31] (Clopper--Pearson) at the D1 boundary, where the rule rejects in no replication. In D2 the
single-seed rates are 0.56 both under the alternative and at the boundary. The paired outcomes of
the rule over the 25 replications of D2 are 15 rejections of both $G^0$ and $G^*$, 10 rejections of
neither and none of only one (\cref{tab:manual-e5-split-paired}). The nominal threshold hides a
clear separation. All 15 rejected alternatives have $\pmed\le7.8\times10^{-6}$, below the
smallest of the 25 boundary values ($3.8\times10^{-5}$), so a critical value just below that
value rejects the alternative in 15 of the 25 replications and the boundary in none, and within the
same replication the alternative $\pmed$ is below the boundary $\pmed$ in 21 of the 25. At the
main split, over the 100 replications of D2, the rule rejects both in 8, only the alternative in 37
and neither in 55, so there the 20\% volatility error adds detections beyond the boundary
rejections at the nominal threshold as well; the 45 rejected alternatives all lie below the
smallest of the 100 boundary values ($2.0\times10^{-4}$), and the alternative $\pmed$ is below the
boundary $\pmed$ of the same replication in all 100.

\subsection{Optimiser settings on simulated data}

\Cref{tab:e5-simpower} uses two E2 alternatives with independent windows, $n=500$ windows of $L=20$
per sample, a random 50/50 split and the exact permutation p-value with $B=1000$. In (a) both
samples are Student-$t_5$ with daily volatility 0.02 and 0.022. In (c) both are \GARCHt{} with
persistence 0.98 and 0.90, matched unconditional variance and degrees of freedom. Every cell uses
the same samples and the same test randomness in each replication, so cells can be compared on
the paired rejections; we use exact McNemar (binomial) tests on the discordant pairs
\citep{mcnemar_1947_note}. Against the volatility shift, rate $10^{-4}$ at 1000 steps rejects in
19 replications in which the default does not and the reverse never happens (exact
$p<10^{-5}$); it also beats rate $10^{-4}$ at 500 steps (18 against 0) and at 3000 steps (12
against 1, $p=0.003$). Against clustering the two rates at 1000 steps do not differ (8 against
6, $p=0.79$), but at rate $10^{-4}$ 1000 steps beat 500 steps (12 against 0, $p<0.001$). At the
default rate the power changes little with the number of steps (0.44, 0.40 and 0.34 against the
volatility shift; 0.26, 0.30 and 0.30 against clustering), and no difference between two step
counts is significant (smallest exact $p=0.30$). The default cells agree with the \WC{} power at
the same alternatives in \cref{tab:e2-power} (0.30 and 0.34 with $n=500$) within Monte Carlo
error. With 200 instead of 1000 permutations the critic and the statistic are identical in every
replication; the p-values differ by at most 0.039 and 0.080, and the rejection rates by 4 and 0
of 50 replications (4 against 0 and 2 against 2 discordant pairs). One test takes on average 4
to 5\,s with 500 steps, 8 to 10\,s with 1000 steps and 28 to 33\,s with 3000 steps in a
single-threaded worker.

The result does not support the check run that led to the default rate of $10^{-3}$
(\cref{sec:method-critic}): at 1000 steps the rate of \citet{gulrajani2017improved} is at least as
powerful against both alternatives here, and much more powerful against the volatility shift. The
default was fixed before any E2 or real-data run and was kept, because re-tuning on the
alternatives used to evaluate the test would select the test on its evaluation data. The level of
every cell is exact regardless (\cref{prop:exact-level}).

\subsection{Critic architecture}

The ablation replaces only the network of the univariate critic and keeps every other setting of
the \WC{} test (standardisation, objective \eqref{eq:wgan-gp} with $\lgp=10$, Adam with rate
$10^{-3}$, 1000 steps, batch 128, 50/50 split, $B=1000$ permutations). The default is the
multilayer perceptron of \cref{sec:method-critic}. The alternative is a time-pooled
one-dimensional CNN: two convolutions with 64 channels and kernel 5 (``same'' padding), each
followed by a LeakyReLU(0.2), an average of the feature map over the $L$ positions, a dense layer
of width 64, a LeakyReLU and a linear output. The critic is therefore the average over the window
of one learned function of local patches of nine days. It is invariant to where in the window a
local pattern occurs and pools the evidence of every day of the window, as a test on pooled
returns does, while still seeing short-range dependence such as volatility clustering within its
receptive field. The architecture was chosen after the real-data results of the perceptron were
known, and after a pilot on four E2 designs at $n=500$ in which it was far more powerful than the
perceptron at the same size. The pilot is recorded only in the change log of the protocol, which
does not list its designs; its results are not part of the reported runs. The ablation is
therefore post hoc in two respects: the pre-specified verdicts of \cref{sec:emp} remain those of
the perceptron, and the simulated comparison below is partly made on the experiment and sample
size on which the network was selected. The argument of the previous subsection against
re-tuning on the evaluation designs applies to this choice as well.

Every replication reuses the windows and the test randomness of the same replication of E2 or
E2-dep, so the two networks are compared replication by replication (exact McNemar test on the
discordant replications). On simulated data (\cref{tab:e5-arch}) the size of the pooled critic is
0.06 [0.03, 0.10] and 0.05 [0.03, 0.09] in the two null designs (200 replications each), as
\cref{prop:exact-level} requires, and its power is higher than that of the perceptron in every
alternative design, significantly so in all but the block design of E2-dep, where the perceptron
already rejects at 0.86. The gains are largest where the perceptron was weakest: 0.10 to 0.98
against Student-$t_{10}$ windows when the other sample is $t_4$, 0.10 to 0.92 when every
synthetic window is GBM instead of Merton, 0.38 to 0.96 against a 10\% volatility increase and
0.36 to 0.88 against persistence 0.90. Against $t_{10}$ tails and all-GBM windows the pooled
critic far exceeds MMD and nearly matches pooled KS (0.98 and 0.92, against 0.08 and 0.06 for MMD
and 1.00 for KS); with half of the synthetic windows GBM it rejects at 0.16, against 0.06 for MMD
and 0.30 for KS. Against the volatility level it is close to both (0.44 against 0.30 and 0.40 at
a 5\% increase, 0.96 against 0.98 and 1.00 at 10\%). In the dependence-only designs,
where KS has no power by construction, it rejects at 0.90 and 0.96, above MMD (0.70 and 0.78).
Because the network was selected on E2 designs at $n=500$, the gains in the ten E2 cells are
optimistic. The two E2-dep cells ($n=2000$) lie outside the recorded pilot and are the closest
to held-out evidence: there the power rises from 0.46 to 0.90 in the mixture design and from 0.86
to 0.96 in the block design, the latter not significantly (exact McNemar $p=0.18$). The
comparison of \cref{sec:sim} is therefore a statement about the default perceptron, not about
learned critics in general: in the E2 designs, the kind on which the alternative network was
selected, the weakness of the \WC{} test against marginal deviations is largely an architectural
one. The price is computation: on average 66\,s
per test against 9\,s for the perceptron on one CPU thread.

On the S\&P~500 panel (\cref{tab:e5-arch-real}) the pooled critic, run with E3's fitted models,
seeds and test streams, gives the same five decisions: GBM, Merton and Heston \BAD{}, \GARCHt{}
\INCONCL{}, no verdict for the bootstrap. In the 20 replications of D1 and D2 that it shares with
the perceptron, the two networks reach the same decision of the rule in every replication, at the
alternative and at the boundary, so their rule power (1.00 [0.84, 1.00] in D1, 0.45 [0.26, 0.66]
in D2) and boundary size (0.00 [0.00, 0.17] and 0.20 [0.06, 0.44]) coincide. At the D2 boundary these
20 replications differ from the full run: over the 100 replications of E3 the perceptron's
boundary size is 0.08 [0.04, 0.15]. The result is consistent with the real,
non-stationary \TRAIN{} volatility path of D2, rather than the power of the network, keeping the
D2 power below $\bmin$ and \GARCHt{} from a \GOOD{} verdict, but 20 replications cannot establish
it. Two gaps remain. The rule power under learning rate $10^{-4}$, the setting that is more
powerful against the volatility shift in \cref{tab:e5-simpower}, was not estimated (parts gridpower and gridrealvol were not
run). The size of the pooled critic under the dependent protocol of the real data was not
measured, because E1 was run with the perceptron only, so its real-data decisions have no
measured size.
